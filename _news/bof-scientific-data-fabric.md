## NSDF Team to Lead SC26 Discussion on a Scientific Data Fabric for AI-Driven Discovery ##
The <a href= "https://nationalsciencedatafabric.org" target="_blank">National Science Data Fabric (NSDF)</a> leadership team will engage the scientific community at SC26 through a Birds of a Feather (BoF) session titled “Building the Scientific Data Fabric Community for AI-Driven Discovery.” The session is part of the ongoing work of the NSF-funded NSDF to connect scientific infrastructure and broaden access to data.  

On Sept. 1, 2026, the NSDF transitioned from a pilot project to a production-grade national cyberinfrastructure and service supported by the <a href= "https://www.nsf.gov" target="_blank">National Science Foundation</a> with a mission to democratize big data by making it more accessible for scientific discovery. The BoF session will bring together researchers, facility operators, cyberinfrastructure developers, and domain scientists to deepen understanding of that mission and explore the challenges involved in building a more connected scientific data ecosystem.  

NSDF Principal Investigator and Co-founder <a href= "https://eecs.utk.edu/people/michela-taufer/" target="_blank">Michela Taufer</a> of the University of Tennessee will lead the session alongside Co-PI and Co-founder <a href= "https://sci.utah.edu/people/pascucci/" target="_blank">Valerio Pascucci</a> of the University of Utah; Co-PI X. <a href= "https://www.rcac.purdue.edu/about/staff/cxsong" target="_blank">Carol Song</a> of Purdue University; Co-PI <a href= "https://tacc.utexas.edu/about/staff-directory/virginia-trueheart/" target="_blank">Virginia Trueheart</a> of the Texas Advanced Computing Center; and <a href= "https://mlcommons.org/about-us/leadership/" target="_blank">David Kanter</a>, Head of MLPerf at MLCommons, whose benchmarking tools are used by the NSDF.  

The panel will explore how modern research increasingly requires instruments, data repositories, workflows, AI services, edge devices, HPC systems, and scientists to work together across institutional boundaries. Drawing on examples from astronomy, geosciences, materials science, medicine, and high-energy physics, participants will identify common infrastructure challenges and help define a roadmap for interoperable, federated scientific data ecosystems.  

Through the discussion, the NSDF leadership aims to engage the broader scientific community in examining how federated data architectures and autonomous AI could reshape the ways scientific data are accessed, shared, and used for discovery.  

  


**Session details:** Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026, 5:15–6:45 p.m. CST, W190b.
